Founder Wealth Simulator · the delay curve

How long should you wait before raising?

Bootstrapping is "delay forever". Startup school is "raise on day one". Everything below holds the ladder, the terms and the ambition fixed, and varies only when the founder first takes money.

But the risk of dying is U-shaped

Waiting a little is the safest thing you can do. Waiting for profitability is the most dangerous — not because profitability is bad, but because half the cohort never gets there and dies trying.

The claim that survives

Profitability is the thing that stops you dying. Across every trigger, the companies that reach it fail at 7–17%, against 30–52% for their cohort as a whole. That is the largest single effect on this page, and it is the "infinite runway" argument stated as a number.

And raising works against it. Founders who never raise reach profitability 61% of the time, by year 3. Founders who raise on day one reach it 41% of the time, by year 7. Taking money does not merely postpone profitability — it reduces how many companies ever get there at all.

Small-cap configuration — companies topping out near $50M of revenue, grounded on 2025–26 round sizes and lower-middle-market exit multiples. Every figure is a median across 6 replicates of 5,000 paired founders: the same person, same idea, same luck, down each path.
