Founder Wealth Simulator · head to head

Venture capital against bootstrapping, for the same founder

Five thousand founders, each run down both paths with the same idea and the same luck, over twelve years. The venture founder ends up with more money. Almost none of it comes from owning the company.

The result

More money, worthless shares

Because both paths are run for the same founder — same market, same product/market fit, same skill, same annual luck — every number here is a paired difference. Nothing is comparing one population against another.

The founder's ledger splits cleanly in two, and the halves answer different questions. The wage is what you were paid for twelve years of work, minus the salaried job you turned down. Ownership is what the shares actually returned: distributions along the way, plus whatever survived the liquidation preference stack at the end.

Raising at every gate you clear leaves the median founder $0.59M ahead. Split it apart and every dollar is wages: the ownership gap is −$0.02M, and fewer than half of those founders (47.8%) did better on ownership than they would have alone.

Only the founders who take the largest round available every time get a real ownership gain — +$1.39M — and even for them, 40% of the total advantage is still the paycheck.

Distribution

Twelve years later, where does everyone land?

Averages are useless here — venture outcomes are power-law and bootstrapped outcomes are not. This is the whole cohort, sorted into bands. Everything left of the centre line did worse than taking a salaried job for the same twelve years.

The mechanism

Venture equity is a step function. Bootstrapped equity is a ramp.

Sort founders by how good they actually were — product/market fit multiplied by execution skill, both drawn at founding and never observed by anyone — and the two paths pay ownership in completely different shapes.

A bootstrapper's shares are worth something almost immediately and rise steadily, because there is nobody standing in front of them at the exit. The venture founder's shares are worth exactly zero until roughly the 55th percentile of quality, then rise very fast.

The ledger

Follow the money for the median founder

Three sources make up everything a founder banks. The venture founder is paid 3.2× more salary. Their median exit — the thing the whole argument is supposedly about — pays them a third of what the bootstrapper's does.

Everything else

The full comparison

Every figure is the mean across 8 replicates of 5,000 paired founders. Wealth is present value, after tax, net of forgone salary unless noted.

What this is. A simulation, not data. Its value is the counterfactual — the same founder down both paths — which no observational study can produce, because the founders who raise are not the founders who don't.

What to distrust. The model produces too few companies that return less than the capital they raised (49.7% against a real ~70%), which flatters the venture arm. Two parameters nobody has measured — how noisy investor funding decisions are, and how much being understaffed hurts a lean company — move the venture advantage more than the headline does. And an earlier run was inspected before a defect was found in it, so the parameters were re-fitted after a result had been seen.
